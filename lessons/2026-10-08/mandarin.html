🗣️ Mandarin Day 5: Time and Days 时间

Kevin, today is time: today, tomorrow and yesterday, now, what time it is, the week, the evening, and the word for time itself. Combined with your Day 2 numbers, these let you make plans and ask when things happen. Eight words, each with a sentence you could use to book an appointment or set up dinner. Characters are Simplified Chinese. The pronunciation line is Hanyu Pinyin with tone marks.

How to read the tones:
  • 1st ā high and flat · 2nd á rising · 3rd ǎ dipping · 4th à falling. No mark means a short, light (neutral) syllable.
  • The 天 family: 天 tiān means day. 今天 jīntiān (today) is two flat 1st tones; 明天 míngtiān (tomorrow) and 昨天 zuótiān (yesterday) both start with a rising 2nd tone, so listen for the first consonant: m for tomorrow, z for yesterday.
  • Days of the week are just numbers: 星期 xīngqī + 一 to 六 gives Monday to Saturday: 星期一 xīngqīyī Monday … 星期四 xīngqīsì Thursday (today!). Sunday is the exception: 星期天 xīngqītiān (or 星期日 xīngqīrì).
  • Telling time: 点 diǎn means o'clock, so 八点 bā diǎn is eight o'clock. 几点 jǐ diǎn is two 3rd tones, so in speech the first one rises: it sounds like jí diǎn. Pinyin still writes jǐ diǎn. Two o'clock uses 两: 两点 liǎng diǎn.
  • Neutral tones and 一: in 晚上 wǎnshang the 上 is light and short. In 一个星期 the 一 is yí, because 个 is really a 4th tone gè (said lightly as ge), the Day 2 rule again.
  • Handy extras in the examples: 见 jiàn see (明天见 see you tomorrow, from Day 1's 再见), 忙 máng busy, 有 yǒu have, 吗 ma turns a statement into a yes/no question, 天 tiān day. Time words go before the verb: 晚上八点见, not 见晚上八点.
