🗣️ Mandarin Day 2: Numbers 1 to 10 数字

Kevin, today is counting. Ten numbers, one to ten, each with a short real-life example: times, prices, and counting things. Characters are Simplified Chinese. The pronunciation line is Hanyu Pinyin with tone marks. Once you have these ten, you can already say any number up to 99.

How to read the tones:
  • 1st ā high and flat · 2nd á rising · 3rd ǎ dipping · 4th à falling. No mark means a short, light (neutral) syllable.
  • 一 changes: on its own or when counting it is yī. Before a 4th tone it becomes yí (一个 yí ge, because 个 is really gè). Before tones 1, 2, or 3 it becomes yì. Type the plain yī for the word itself.
  • Bigger numbers are easy: 十一 shíyī is 11 (ten-one), 二十 èrshí is 20 (two-ten), and 九十九 jiǔshíjiǔ is 99.
  • 二 vs 两: say 二 èr when counting or in maths. Before a measure word (two people, two apples) Mandarin uses 两 liǎng. That one comes in a later lesson.
  • Phone numbers: in Mainland phone and room numbers, 1 is often read yāo so it is not confused with 7 (qī).
  • Watch these pairs: 四 sì (four) vs 十 shí (ten). The s is a plain s, the sh curls the tongue back. Tones differ too: falling vs rising.
