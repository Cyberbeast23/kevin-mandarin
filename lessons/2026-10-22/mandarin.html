🗣️ Mandarin Day 19: Ordering at a Restaurant 点菜

Kevin, it's Thursday, and yesterday you made plans to eat together, so today you sit down at the restaurant: the menu, calling the server, ordering, chopsticks, a bowl of noodles, food that's too spicy, and asking for the bill. The cover word is 点菜 diǎn cài — to order food. Old friends come back: 好吃, 水, 面条 and 要 from food and shopping, 请 from greetings, 现在 from time, and 给 and 吧 from yesterday. Characters are Simplified Chinese. The pronunciation line is Hanyu Pinyin with tone marks.

How to read the tones:
  • 1st ā high and flat · 2nd á rising · 3rd ǎ dipping · 4th à falling. No mark means a short, light (neutral) syllable.
  • 一 with a measure word: before a 1st or 3rd tone, 一 becomes 4th, so 一碗 is yì wǎn (one bowl) and 一杯 is yì bēi (one glass or cup).
  • Half 3rd tone: a 3rd tone before another tone usually just stays low without rising back up, so 买单 mǎidān is low then high, and 点菜 diǎn cài is low then falling.
  • 筷子 ends light: 子 is neutral, so kuàizi is a strong fall then a short tap.
  • 服务员 has three beats: fúwùyuán rises, falls, then rises again. Calling 服务员！ is the normal, polite way to get a server's attention in China.
  • 辣 is one sharp fall: là. 不辣 bú là means not spicy (不 becomes 2nd before a 4th tone), and 有点辣 yǒudiǎn là is a little spicy.
  • Handy extras in the examples: 家 jiā (counting word for restaurants and shops), 杯 bēi glass or cup, 会 huì can (know how to), 用 yòng to use, 菜 cài a dish, 太…了 tài…le too (much).
