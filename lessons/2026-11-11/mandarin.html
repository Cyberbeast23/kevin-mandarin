🗣️ Mandarin Day 39: Grocery Shopping 去超市买菜

Kevin, today we push a cart around the supermarket: fruit, apples, bananas, milk, bread, buying by the 斤 jīn, checking things are fresh, and grabbing a shopping cart. Spot the pattern: 果 guǒ (fruit) ends both 水果 and 苹果. The phrase to keep is 苹果多少钱一斤？ Píngguǒ duōshao qián yì jīn?, How much are the apples per jin? That is exactly how prices are asked at a Chinese market. Old friends come back: 超市, 买, 多少钱, 便宜, 早饭, 蔬菜, and 甜. Characters are Simplified Chinese. The pronunciation line is Hanyu Pinyin with tone marks.

How to read the tones:
  • 1st ā high and flat · 2nd á rising · 3rd ǎ dipping · 4th à falling. No mark means a short, light (neutral) syllable.
  • Two dips in a row: 水果 is written shuǐguǒ (3-3), but you say the first one rising: shuí-guǒ. Two 3rd tones together always do this.
  • Rise, then dip: 苹果 píngguǒ is 2-3, and 牛奶 niúnǎi is 2-3 too.
  • Flat and high: 香蕉 xiāngjiāo is 1-1, and 新鲜 xīnxiān is 1-1. Keep your voice up and steady for both syllables.
  • Fall, then high: 面包 miànbāo is 4-1.
  • Fall, fall, high: 购物车 gòuwùchē is 4-4-1. Literally buy-things-cart.
  • The market weight: 斤 jīn is 1st tone, about 500 grams. Price comes first: 多少钱一斤 means how much per jin.
  • Handy extras: 买菜 mǎi cài means grocery shopping; 这些 zhèxiē means these; 拿 ná means grab or take.
